# Perspective source brief

## Intended contribution

Write a 1,500-1,800 word cross-disciplinary Perspective for researchers who
publish machine-learning benchmark results. The thesis is that leaderboards
should report performance uncertainty and rank instability alongside point
ranks because small observed score gaps often do not establish a stable model
ordering.

The piece should make one constructive proposal: a minimal reporting panel
containing the evaluation unit, resampling or repeated-run procedure, score
intervals, pairwise uncertainty for close competitors, and a rank-stability
summary. It should not argue that rankings are never useful or require a single
statistical test for every benchmark.

## Frozen evidence packet

1. Demsar J. Statistical comparisons of classifiers over multiple data sets.
   *Journal of Machine Learning Research*. 2006;7:1-30.
   https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v7/demsar06a.html
   Relevant support: comparisons across datasets require analysis that respects
   the dataset as the comparison unit and accounts for multiple classifiers.
2. Reimers N, Gurevych I. Reporting score distributions makes a difference:
   Performance study of LSTM-networks for sequence tagging. *EMNLP*. 2017.
   https://aclanthology.org/D17-1035/
   Relevant support: repeated runs can yield score distributions whose overlap
   changes conclusions drawn from single runs.
3. Dror R, Baumer G, Shlomov S, Reichart R. The hitchhiker's guide to testing
   statistical significance in natural language processing. *ACL*. 2018.
   https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/
   Relevant support: the valid test depends on assumptions, evaluation design,
   and the relation between samples.
4. Dodge J, Ilharco G, Schwartz R, Farhadi A, Hajishirzi H, Smith N. Fine-tuning
   pretrained language models: Weight initializations, data orders, and early
   stopping. 2020.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06305
   Relevant support: training choices and random variation can affect reported
   downstream results.

Treat these as a bounded starting corpus. Verify identity before making a
material claim and weaken or remove any statement the sources do not support.
Do not infer that the cited studies cover every field or every source of
benchmark uncertainty.

## Argument boundary and counter-position

The strongest counter-position is that compact leaderboards lower reader
burden, repeated evaluation can be expensive, and uncertainty displays can
obscure the benchmark's operational purpose. Address this by proposing a
minimum disclosure proportional to the decision: inexpensive paired
resampling when predictions are available, repeated training when training
stochasticity is material, and explicit limitation when neither is feasible.

Do not equate statistical uncertainty with practical importance. Do not claim
that overlapping marginal intervals prove equivalence. Do not prescribe a
universal alpha threshold. The article should distinguish uncertainty in a
score, uncertainty in a pairwise difference, and instability in an ordering.
