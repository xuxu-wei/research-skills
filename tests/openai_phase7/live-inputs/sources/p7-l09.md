# Completed research dossier source

## Completed work

The team completed a controlled fault-injection study comparing three retry
policies in a replicated key-value service: fixed delay, exponential backoff,
and load-aware backoff. Forty prespecified fault patterns crossed packet loss,
latency spikes, one-node restarts, and two-node rolling restarts. Every policy
was tested with the same randomized trace order and five independent repeats.

Primary outcome: time to restore 99% successful requests after fault onset.
Secondary outcomes: peak retry traffic, failed requests during recovery, and
steady-state p95 latency. Analyses used the fault pattern as the comparison
unit and paired policy contrasts with bootstrap intervals.

## Locked results and present framing

- Median recovery time was 42 s for fixed delay, 31 s for exponential backoff,
  and 24 s for load-aware backoff.
- Relative to exponential backoff, load-aware backoff reduced recovery time by
  6.8 s on average (95% CI 3.1 to 10.2) and peak retry traffic by 18% (95% CI
  11% to 25%).
- The improvement was concentrated in high-loss and rolling-restart patterns;
  the policies were similar under isolated latency spikes.
- Steady-state p95 latency differed by less than 2 ms among policies, with
  intervals spanning zero.
- All 40 fault patterns, run logs, configuration files, analysis code, and a
  reproducible container are available. The current draft frames the work only
  as "a faster retry algorithm" and gives little attention to conditional
  effects, operational decision rules, or reproducible resilience testing.

The findings apply to the tested service, topology, loads, and fault library.
They do not establish behavior in geographically distributed deployment,
Byzantine faults, or other storage engines.
