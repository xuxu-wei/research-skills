# Completed computational benchmark record

## Article objective

Prepare a full research article for a generic peer-reviewed software-systems
outlet from the completed study below. Target-specific instructions have not

## Research question and design

The study asked whether cache-aware request batching reduces energy per 10,000
processed records relative to fixed-size batching without worsening tail
latency. Two x86 servers with identical processors, memory, operating-system
image, and application container were tested. Each server completed 40 paired
blocks. Within each block, the order of the two batching policies was randomized
and both policies processed the same replay trace.

The primary endpoint was wall-plug energy in kJ per 10,000 successfully
processed records. Prespecified secondary endpoints were p95 latency in ms and
throughput in records/s. Server and paired block were retained in the analysis;
time points within a run were not treated as independent replicates. The plan
used paired mean differences with a cluster bootstrap over blocks, two-sided
95% confidence intervals, and no multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory claims for
secondary endpoints.

## Reproducibility facts

- Application version: 3.8.2; container digest and dependency lockfile frozen.
- Replay set: 12 traces stratified by request-size distribution; every trace
  appears in both policies on both servers.
- Power meter sampled at 1 Hz and was calibrated before the experiment.
- Four runs failed the prespecified integrity check before outcome inspection
  and were rerun under the original randomized assignment.
- The complete run ledger contains 80 paired blocks. Analysis code reproduces
  the final table from the locked CSV; their SHA-256 values are recorded in the
  team's archive manifest.

## Locked results

| Endpoint | Fixed-size batching | Cache-aware batching | Paired difference (cache-aware minus fixed) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Energy per 10,000 records | 12.4 kJ | 9.8 kJ | -2.6 kJ (95% CI -3.0 to -2.2) |
| p95 latency | 184 ms | 181 ms | -3 ms (95% CI -8 to 2) |
| Throughput | 545 records/s | 672 records/s | +127 (95% CI 112 to 142) |

Server-specific energy differences were -2.5 and -2.7 kJ. Trace-stratified
estimates had the same direction; the smallest reduction was 1.8 kJ for the
lowest-load trace. No outcome was measured for different hardware, applications,
or live production traffic.

## Claim boundary

The source supports a claim about this application, hardware class, replay
set, and experimental configuration. It does not establish lifecycle carbon
impact, cost savings, universal latency equivalence, or production reliability.
The neutral interpretation is that the tested policy reduced energy and
increased throughput while the p95-latency interval was compatible with a
small improvement or small worsening.
