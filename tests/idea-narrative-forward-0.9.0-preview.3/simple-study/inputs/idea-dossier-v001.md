# Agreement of a portable nitrate assay with ion chromatography in river water

## Title, summary, audience, and positioning

- **Title:** Agreement of a portable nitrate assay with ion chromatography in river water
- **One-sentence complete-Idea summary:** This study will compare a portable colorimetric nitrate assay with ion chromatography in river-water samples to estimate bias, agreement, and repeatability and define the concentration range in which the assay can support field screening.
- **Primary audience:** Environmental chemistry and laboratory quality researchers.
- **Positioning and contribution frame:** A bounded local validation study of an existing measurement method.

## Structured abstract

River monitoring programs need rapid nitrate measurements, but field assays may be affected by sample matrix and operator variation. We will compare one portable colorimetric assay with laboratory ion chromatography across the concentration range encountered in local rivers. The study will estimate bias, agreement, and repeatability and will identify the range in which the assay is suitable for screening. The result will be a bounded measurement-validity claim, not a claim that the portable assay can replace regulatory laboratory testing.

## Background, current state, gap, significance, and rationale

### Background

Nitrate monitoring supports the detection of nutrient pollution in rivers. Ion chromatography provides a laboratory reference measurement but is poorly suited to immediate field decisions.

### Current state

Portable colorimetric assays offer faster results and require less equipment. Their performance depends on concentration, water matrix, and operator handling, so manufacturer specifications alone do not establish performance in local river samples.

### Gap

The agreement and repeatability of the selected portable assay have not been quantified across the nitrate range and sample matrices represented in the intended monitoring program.

### Significance

A defensible screening range would let monitoring teams use rapid measurements where they are reliable and send uncertain samples for laboratory testing, reducing delay without weakening measurement quality.

### Rationale

A paired comparison against ion chromatography, combined with replicate portable measurements, directly estimates systematic bias, concentration-dependent disagreement, and within-sample repeatability in the intended samples.

## Research question, objectives, and core hypothesis

In river-water samples, what bias, limits of agreement, and repeatability does the portable nitrate assay show relative to ion chromatography? The objective is to define its supported screening range. We expect acceptable agreement in the mid-range and wider disagreement near the detection limits.

## Research content and work packages

Collect samples across sites and seasons; obtain paired reference and portable measurements; repeat the portable measurement; estimate agreement and repeatability; define the supported screening range.

## Data, materials, and existing evidence base

The study will use 120 archived and prospectively collected river-water samples, the selected portable assay, and an accredited ion-chromatography laboratory. Manufacturer documentation supports assay operation but not the local agreement claim.

## Research design and methods

Use a paired measurement-comparison design. Blind portable-assay operators to reference results. Randomize sample order. Estimate mean bias and concentration-dependent bias, Bland–Altman limits of agreement, and within-sample coefficient of variation with confidence intervals.

## Key techniques and implementation

Use a prespecified calibration check, duplicate portable measurements, standardized sample storage, and a reproducible analysis script. Report invalid runs and missing measurements without substitution.

## Required analyses and evidence

Report sample coverage, missing and invalid measurements, calibration checks, bias with confidence intervals, limits of agreement, repeatability, and sensitivity analyses by concentration band and broad matrix class.

## Expected outputs, falsification criteria, and interpretations

The outputs are a measurement-agreement report, reproducible analysis, and screening-range recommendation. The screening claim is not supported if agreement is materially worse than the prespecified tolerance or if repeatability is inadequate. A failed result still identifies where laboratory testing remains necessary.

## Contribution, innovation, impact, application, and closest-work comparison

The contribution is local validation of an existing assay for a defined screening use. The study does not claim a new assay or analytical method. Its value is a transparent decision boundary for field monitoring.

## Feasibility, resources, risks, alternatives, and stop conditions

### Feasibility and resources

The instruments, laboratory access, trained operators, and target sample count are available within six months.

### Working assumptions (omit when none)

The archived samples have storage records adequate for the planned sensitivity analysis.

### Limitations and boundary conditions (omit when none)

Results apply to the selected assay, sampled rivers, observed matrices, and concentration range. Agreement does not establish interchangeability with regulatory laboratory testing.

### Risks, alternatives, and stop conditions

If calibration checks fail repeatedly, pause collection and service the instrument. If the concentration range is too narrow, add targeted samples before estimating the screening range. Stop the screening claim if the prespecified agreement tolerance is not met.
