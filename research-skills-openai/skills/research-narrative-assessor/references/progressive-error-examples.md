# Progressive narrative error examples

## Use boundary

Read only an entry matching a category already observed during the rubric pass. Each
entry distinguishes functions and false positives. Do not match literal phrases, count
occurrences, impose sentence-length thresholds, or treat an example as required wording.

## Motivation displaced by defense

- Reader harm: qualifications occupy the function that should establish the problem,
  value, or thesis.
- Positive boundary: a boundary appears where it distinguishes the target question
  from a materially different one and enables the next reasoning step.
- Error boundary: removing defensive material reveals that no positive motivation or
  thesis remains.

## Concept before problem

- Reader harm: the reader must learn a construct before knowing the explanatory job it
  performs.
- Positive boundary: the construct may come first when it is itself the study object
  and its definition immediately establishes the problem.
- Error boundary: the construct precedes both the unresolved phenomenon and the reason
  it is needed.

## Section-function substitution

- Reader harm: a heading promises one decision-relevant function but supplies another.
- Positive boundary: one passage may perform adjacent functions when both are explicit
  and their relation is intelligible.
- Error boundary: the section cannot answer its promised question without importing an
  essential answer from elsewhere.

## Repetition without a distinct function

- Reader harm: multiple passages compete as authorities and obscure the bounded claim.
- Positive boundary: repeated mention is necessary when each occurrence supplies a
  different required input, output, decision, or interpretation.
- Error boundary: the repeated passage adds no local function and preserves no distinct
  scientific condition.

## Additive-only repair

- Reader harm: new explanation accumulates beside obsolete, competing, or duplicate
  material.
- Positive boundary: addition alone is valid when a missing function is localized and
  no existing text competes with it.
- Error boundary: the proposed action cannot identify what will be replaced, moved,
  merged, consolidated, or deleted.

## Technically precise but unreadable

- Reader harm: all conditions exist, but their order forces reconstruction.
- Positive boundary: difficult subject matter is not a narrative defect when
  prerequisites are explicit, definitions arrive at need, and no simpler faithful
  route exists.
- Error boundary: consequences precede the premises needed to interpret them.

## Reader-baseline mismatch

- Reader harm: the argument assumes expertise that one or more declared reader groups
  were not assigned, so central reasoning depends on unexplained disciplinary knowledge.
- Positive boundary: a specialized term or inference may remain compact when every
  declared reader group can reasonably know it or the needed premise has already been
  established.
- Error boundary: a central step is intelligible only to one discipline and no prior
  explanation or bridge supplies the missing premise to the others.

## Workflow-control language displaces science

- Reader harm: internal process labels become the apparent subject of the research
  story, forcing readers to translate project control into scientific meaning.
- Positive boundary: a procedural term remains when it is itself a recognized part of
  the study design and is defined by its scientific function.
- Error boundary: replacing the internal label with the actual condition, decision, or
  consequence reveals the scientific relation that the prose had obscured.

## Qualification saturation

- Reader harm: caveats and exclusions occupy the space needed to state the question,
  answer, contribution, or next reasoning step.
- Positive boundary: a qualification stays local when it is necessary to interpret the
  immediately adjacent claim and omission would materially mislead.
- Error boundary: the reader encounters repeated boundaries before learning the
  positive claim they bound, or the same complete limitation competes across locations.
