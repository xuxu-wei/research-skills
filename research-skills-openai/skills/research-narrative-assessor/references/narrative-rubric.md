# Narrative rubric

Assess functions and dependencies, not scientific quality or local language polish.

## 1. Reader reasoning

The selected profile's reasoning chain must be present, distinct, and connected. A
list of nearby facts is not a chain. A novelty comparison is not a scientific gap. A
method description is not a rationale unless it explains why that method can address
the stated gap.

## 2. Section-function fit

Each required section performs the function promised by its heading before adding
detail. Required sections may share facts, but they do not become duplicates when each
performs a distinct question, design, evidence, interpretation, or audit function.

## 3. Progressive disclosure

Introduce the problem and required premises before constructs, conditions, or
consequences that depend on them. Judge prerequisite knowledge against the declared
reader handoff. Technical density is acceptable when irreducible and properly ordered.

## 4. Core-element alignment

Titles, summaries, questions or theses, objectives or argument steps, evidence, and
conclusions must lead to the same scientific object and bounded contribution. Route
scientific disagreements to the appropriate evaluator rather than resolving them.

## 5. Narrative authority and repetition

Each complete limitation family has one authority location. Elsewhere, retain only a
self-contained boundary that directly enables immediate reasoning and whose omission
would distort it. Do not add pointers. In a Perspective, apply this rule separately to
each counterargument or boundary family.

Flag repetition only when it adds no distinct reader function. Repair should identify
what is replaced, moved, merged, consolidated, or deleted; additive repair is justified
only for a genuinely missing function.

## 6. Navigation and backtracking

Readers should not need later sections to decode earlier central claims. A forward
reference is acceptable when the earlier statement is complete at its own level and
the later material supplies optional depth.

## 7. Terminology boundary

This assessor may identify that a concept arrives too early or lacks a needed
reader-facing definition at a named location. It must not decide whether a term is
standard, verify a translation, or supply exact wording. Those are language-assessment
tasks.

## Decisions

- `narrative_ready`: no narrative repair action is required.
- `minor_narrative_revision`: localized action resolves every finding without changing
  the reader's main route.
- `major_narrative_revision`: a reasoning function is missing or broken, or material
  restructuring, consolidation, or alignment is required.
- `clarification_required`: missing or conflicting intent prevents an executable
  editorial target.

Severity follows reader-function damage, not word choice, sentence length, finding
count, or repeated tokens.
