# Review considerations

Read when assessing a statistical analysis plan.

- **Alignment:** Do the estimands, populations, endpoints, time windows, and comparisons answer the protocol's scientific questions?
- **Data support:** Are the necessary variables, observations, and measurement timings available or credibly planned? Are derivations and exclusions clear?
- **Methods:** Do the models, covariates, dependence structure, and assumptions suit the design and intended inference?
- **Uncertainty:** Are missingness, multiplicity, sample information, and sensitivity analyses addressed where they could alter interpretation?
- **Implementability:** Can an analyst follow the important decisions without choosing methods according to favorable results? Are assumptions, diagnostics, and contingencies usable?
- **Transparency:** Are primary and exploratory analyses distinguished, and are amendments consistent with when the results became known?

Do not prescribe a familiar method solely because it is conventional. Explain why a proposed alternative better serves the scientific question. A plan may be useful at an early stage without resolving every implementation detail, but uncertainty affecting its central inference must remain visible.
