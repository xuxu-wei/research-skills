# Improvement considerations

Read when comparing ways to strengthen an existing study.

## Establish the baseline

Describe the actual dataset, design, analyses, findings, limitations, and current contribution. Distinguish an underexplained finding from an unsupported claim. A clearer narrative is valuable when it improves understanding, but it does not repair a missing comparison or invalid measurement.

## Identify meaningful changes

Consider whether the study would benefit from a better-defined question, more defensible interpretation, robustness analysis, external validation, improved measurement, or a bounded extension. New work should address a consequential uncertainty rather than merely make the project larger.

For each option, state what new information it could provide and whether that information would change the scientific conclusion. Further analysis of the same data may improve understanding without providing independent confirmation. Post hoc reframing and exploratory analyses should remain identifiable as such.

## Compare feasibility and value

Account for available variables, information content, access, expertise, time, approvals, and budget. Distinguish an estimate from a verified commitment. Avoid precision in costs or benefits that the available evidence cannot support.

Include failure risks and useful stopping points. A null finding or unsuccessful extension can still be informative; an option that only adds selective positive results may undermine credibility.

Consider intended readers and an appropriate dissemination route after assessing scientific merit. Assess journal fit only against verified scope and requirements, without acceptance probabilities or a promise that repackaging will improve the evidence.

## Recommend proportionate work

Favor an option whose expected scientific benefit justifies its burden. Explain when a narrower claim, clearer limitations, or no further study work is the sound choice. Keep reasonable alternatives and review disagreements available to the researcher.
