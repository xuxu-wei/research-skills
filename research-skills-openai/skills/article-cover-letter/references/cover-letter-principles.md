# Cover Letter Principles

The cover letter is an editorial triage memo, not a second abstract. Its job is to help an editor decide quickly whether the manuscript has editorial value for the target journal.

## Core Logic

Answer these questions:

- What specific uncertainty or unresolved issue blocks the field?
- What knowledge-state change does this manuscript create?
- Is the innovation incremental or 0-to-1?
- Why does this delta fit the target journal's readers?
- What credibility signals or disclosures should the editor know?
- What evidence boundaries prevent overclaiming?

## Structure

Default one-page structure:

1. Submission statement and problem positioning.
2. Contribution delta and innovation type.
3. Journal fit and readership value.
4. Credibility, limitations, and disclosures.

Target 350-500 English words unless journal instructions differ.

## Contribution Types

- `reframes`: changes the conceptual framing of a problem.
- `strengthens_inference`: makes an existing judgment more reliable or precise.
- `clarifies_decision`: informs a clinical, policy, diagnostic, or operational decision.
- `enables_measurement`: makes a previously inaccessible construct measurable.
- `defines_boundaries`: shows where an existing claim applies or fails.
- `reorganizes_evidence`: synthesizes or classifies evidence around a more useful distinction.
- `enables_research`: provides a tool, dataset, protocol, or platform for future work.

## Incremental Innovation

Do not write "larger sample" or "confirmed previous findings" as the contribution. Explain what uncertainty became smaller, what bias was addressed, what boundary became clearer, or what decision became more reliable.

Useful verbs: `refines`, `resolves`, `extends`, `distinguishes`, `validates`, `operationalizes`, `strengthens inference`.

## 0-to-1 Innovation

Do not rely on "first study" language. Explain how the manuscript reframes, introduces, defines, reveals, enables, challenges, or opens a new line of inquiry.

## Journal Fit

Avoid generic praise. State why the journal's specific readership needs the manuscript's delta. For biomedical journals, connect fit to clinical decision-making, public health, mechanism, policy, guideline relevance, or patient-important outcomes where applicable.

## Disclosures

Include editor-relevant facts when available: preprint, related manuscripts, prior communication with editors, conflicts of interest, ethics approval, author approval, data/code availability, suggested reviewers, excluded reviewers, and non-duplicate submission statement.

## Anti-Patterns

- Repeating the abstract.
- Listing methods and results without the editorial delta.
- Unsupported "first", "novel", "groundbreaking", or "paradigm-shifting" claims.
- Generic "suitable for your prestigious journal" phrasing.
- Omitting limitations or required disclosures.
