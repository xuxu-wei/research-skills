# Cross-Component Anti-Patterns

Use this shared list when drafting, evaluating, reviewing, and finalizing.

1. Caveat creep: multiple layers of qualification erase the thesis.
2. Review-response language in the article body.
3. Pedagogical rhetorical questions that substitute for argument.
4. Mini-review drift: literature summary replaces perspective.
5. Framework-before-evidence: taxonomy appears before the problem is earned.
6. Narrative vignette opening that does not carry argumentative weight.
7. Weak or indirect evidence supporting strong claims.
8. Orphan paragraph with no argument-step function.
9. Strawman counterargument.
10. Absolute language unsupported by the claim ledger.
11. Outlet mismatch hidden by style polish.
12. Generic-profile readiness overstated as submission readiness.
13. Counterargument/boundary authority duplication across sections.
14. A limitation pointer replaces a necessary self-contained local boundary.
