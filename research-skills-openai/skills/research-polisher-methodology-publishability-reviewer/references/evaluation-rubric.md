# Evaluation rubric

Apply every dimension to each anonymous option. Use qualitative findings; do not invent numeric precision.

## 1. Method and design compatibility

Check whether the proposed claim remains supportable by the frozen design, data, estimand/metric, analysis, comparison, uncertainty, and scope. For added-work options, check that the named work could actually support the claim delta.

## 2. Evidence-claim fit

Trace existing claims to frozen evidence IDs and new claims to explicit added-work items. Reject narrative inflation, causal strengthening, population broadening, mechanism claims, or practical claims without a support route.

## 3. Effort-tier correctness

- `reposition_only`: added-work list is empty and no new result is required.
- `small_extension`: one bounded package, no core-design change, certain/high feasibility with named dependencies.
- `moderate_extension`: one coherent bounded evidence layer, not a standalone study or wholesale redesign.

Misclassification is at least a major finding; hidden substantive work in `reposition_only` rejects the option.

## 4. Feasibility calibration

Check assets, access, resources, technical capacity, timing, fallback, and stop condition against the dossier. Unsupported `certain` or `high` feasibility requires revision or rejection.

## 5. Incremental value

Assess whether the option could materially clarify scientific contribution, practical use, or dissemination relevance without overstating what is known. Distinguish a real contribution delta from cosmetic wording.

## 6. Narrative differentiation

Check whether the story arc is coherent, distinctive, and aligned with the evidence. Reject marketing-only reframing that outruns methods or results.

## 7. Publishability

Assess general outlet archetype, contribution clarity, completeness, likely reviewer concerns, and repairability. Target-specific fit is assessable only with a verified adapter; otherwise use `target_requirements_unverified` and `not_assessed`.

## Fatal findings

Option-level fatal examples: unsupported central claim, infeasible required work, prohibited tier-zero work, or dependence on missing core evidence. Reject only the affected option when the source remains usable.

Source-level fatal examples: the frozen design cannot support any proposed central inference, source identity/digest is inconsistent, or all options depend on a missing core result. These block human selection.

## Pareto axes

Provide comparable qualitative values when supported for effort, feasibility, methodological risk, scientific-significance potential, practical-value potential, dissemination potential, and publication positioning. Do not calculate a weighted total or choose a winner.
