# Focused Terminology Review

Use this reference only after ordinary reading identifies a term that may
impede the stated target reader. This is a focused check inside the Language
Assessment Report, not a separate workflow, artifact, or full terminology
inventory.

## What is a core term

A core term is necessary to understand at least one of the following:

- title or one-sentence summary;
- research question or primary objective;
- main scientific contribution;
- central study object, measurement, inference target, or design relation;
- any term whose meaning controls the primary question, measurement or
  inference, design or analysis choice, validation when present, or scientific
  interpretation.

A merely frequent word is not automatically core. A highly technical term can
be acceptable when it is standard for the target reader or promptly defined.

## Trigger and evidence

After the bounded Idea-dossier scan is run, read every reader-entry unit and use
its compact-form and internal-token candidates only as attention prompts. A
quoted phrase, parenthetical expression, abbreviation, proper name, or compact
form does not by itself trigger focused review. Inspect a candidate in context
only when its referent, reader accessibility, standardity, translation, or
cross-location use is genuinely uncertain. Do not turn the scan into an
exhaustive term inventory, and do not stop the reader-entry pass merely because
one obvious problem has been found. Whole-dossier concordance remains limited
to terms that actually trigger focused review.

Separately inspect every scanner candidate classified as a mixed-language or
internal token when it appears in prose or a free-form reader-facing label.
Treat it as fixed scaffolding, standard/defined language, a direct description,
or an actionable finding; do not persist the passing dispositions.

Treat each reader-entry sentence as an actor--operation--object--criterion check.
Inspect separately the name of the study object or model, the operation asserted
of it, every named measurement or target quantity, and the success, failure, or
interpretation consequence. Do not close the sentence merely because one broad
term family has a finding: every other compact role phrase in that sentence still
needs its own standard, defined, or direct disposition. In particular, wording
such as “recover”, “evaluate”, “update”, or “validate” is not self-explanatory
when its grammatical object remains an undefined project label.

The compact-label group is an attention aid, not a blacklist. A standard and
defined form or a direct description passes without a report entry; an
ambiguous core label receives a finding only after semantic confirmation. Give
the same contextual check to any named result or output that controls a
scientific interpretation; its wording must identify what result, criterion,
or consequence it displays.

After editorial repair, the separate short-form diff is advisory. The writer
records one of `removed`, `standard_and_defined`, `descriptive_not_label`, or
`fixed_scaffolding` for each diff candidate in the existing revision delta.
Candidate presence alone is not a failure. Only a candidate that remains a
confirmed unresolved terminology problem becomes an actionable finding and
blocks readiness. Do not copy resolved dispositions into the fresh Language
Assessment Report, create a term register, or persist the full candidate list.

Verify only a term that appears coined, nonstandard, ambiguous, disputed,
inconsistent across languages, or inaccessible at the supplied reader
baseline. Prefer, in order:

1. domain guidelines, consensus statements, standards, or controlled
   vocabularies;
2. authoritative methodological or reporting guidance;
3. high-quality reviews;
4. representative use by at least two independent research groups; and
5. authoritative bilingual mappings when translation is at issue.

One paper's wording is not sufficient evidence that a phrase is standard. If
verification is unnecessary, do not retrieve sources. If a core term cannot be
verified efficiently, recommend a direct descriptive expression rather than
keeping or inventing a compact label.

Grammatical compositionality alone does not establish standardity. A compact
phrase that names a project-specific construct still requires verification or
a direct description when readers from any stated target discipline would need
to infer its referent, inputs, operation, or role.

Re-run this same trigger, evidence, reader-baseline, and modifier-attachment
check on every proposed replacement. Never replace an unverified compact label
with another compact label inferred only from the dossier. Without adequate
standard-term evidence, the replacement must name the scientific object,
operation, relation, or error directly enough to be understood without a
project glossary. A later short form is optional and must follow that direct
explanation rather than substitute for it.

Also separate scientifically distinct objects, assessments, failure states, and
actions that an awkward literal translation collapses. Give each role a
function-specific standard or plain phrase; sharing one translated word root
does not establish standard usage. Do not split forms that the target discipline
uses as one established concept merely to make wording more explicit. This is a
functional test, not a ban on any particular English or Chinese word.

Apply the same role test to ordinary-looking action nouns and metaphors. A term
is not reader-ready when it alternately names a diagnostic result, a decision,
the status of an analysed object, and a record of that decision. Replace such a
label with wording that identifies the actor or trigger, the affected object,
and the scientific consequence. Also check compact terms that are standard in
only one target subfield but have a misleading everyday or cross-disciplinary
reading; define the exact scientific referent at first use or use a direct
description.

Treat a compact label used in an eligibility, alternative-analysis, stopping,
or interpretation consequence as core when it controls what may be analysed or
claimed. Scan those consequence statements explicitly, even when the label is
an ordinary dictionary word. A proposed definition or acceptance test is not
self-contained if it introduces another undefined compact label; expand that
label or give its referent in the same repair action.

## Composite phrases and title checks

Do not use the absence of an exact full-title string as evidence that a term is
coined or nonstandard. Decompose a composite phrase into its semantic head and
any scope, population, evidence-status, or design modifiers. Verify only the
head or an ambiguous component. A standard semantic head combined with
transparent descriptive modifiers can pass even when the exact full phrase is
not found verbatim in a source. For a standard or descriptive composite phrase,
an immediate explanation in the one-sentence summary can supply the reader
orientation that the title itself cannot contain. These allowances do not
rescue an opaque metaphor, a misleading component, or a genuinely coined title
label.

Also parse modifier attachment in every compound title phrase. If ordinary
syntax makes a property appear to modify the wrong study object, population,
design, or evidence class, treat the phrase as misleading even when each
component is individually standard. Prefer a direct phrase that names the
property-bearing object.

Re-parse every proposed title replacement before recommending it. The repair
itself must make each scope, condition, evidence-status, and data-property
modifier attach unambiguously to its intended head; moving the ambiguity to a
different modifier does not pass.

Treat project-specific stage labels and technical validation labels as core
when they carry the gap, hypothesis, main decision, or contribution. At first
use, name the scientific referent or stage contents and the label's function
before relying on the short form.

Do not equate deferred operational detail with a missing first-use definition.
If a descriptive phrase already lets the stated reader identify its referent
and role, a later formula, input list, loading rule, numerical direction, or
implementation threshold is ordinary progressive detail. Such detail can still
motivate a localized clarity edit, but it fails the terminology hard gate only
when the reader cannot identify the term's referent or function, or when the
wording positively suggests the wrong meaning.

First use means the first reader-facing use of the compact label, not the first
plain-language mention of the underlying component. A summary may state a
component's function without naming the label and defer the label and its full
definition to the structured abstract or relevant method section. Do not force a
technical definition into the summary merely to preserve a short label.

## Choice order

Prefer a domain-standard term, then a widely understood cross-disciplinary
term, then plain descriptive wording. Retain a coined term only when no accurate
standard term exists, the concept genuinely needs a name, the label is natural
rather than an internal metaphor, it is defined immediately, and usage is
consistent. Avoid coined terms in titles; if indispensable in a summary,
explain them in the same sentence.

For a confirmed actionable problem, set `finding_kind: terminology` and report
the exact locator, reader baseline,
problem, term or phrase, recommended verified form or plain description,
first-use definition, evidence basis, every observed competing form and
locator (an explicit empty list when none exist), and an acceptance test. Do not judge the
underlying scientific method, novelty, impact, feasibility, or argument.

Use one `meso`/`concept_cluster` finding when the same scientific role or a
bounded set of easily confused roles has competing forms across locations. Use
`micro`/`occurrence` only when a localized expression can be repaired without a
whole-dossier naming decision. Give the finding a readable scientific-role key,
normalized locator, failure-mode key, and their prescribed fingerprint; never
use a digest. Macro section order or argument function remains outside this
role.

For a concept cluster, map every distinct scientific role to its recommended
verified form or direct description; do not offer one generic instruction for
several target quantities. The required change must state which forms to
replace, which definition appears first, what semantic distinction survives,
and what the whole-dossier concordance check must find. If source evidence does
not support a standard short form, recommend the direct description and do not
invent a replacement label.

When clear wording depends on a scientific or statistical choice that the dossier
explicitly leaves unfrozen, do not choose an option for the writer. Recommend a
plain statement that identifies the pending specification, its allowed decision
point, and any already fixed quantity. If the text cannot do that without changing
the design, return the exact clarification needed.

## Scaffolding and role boundaries

- Required machine frontmatter, lineage keys, contract-fixed headings, schema
  field labels, code identifiers, and mathematical symbols are not evidence of
  reader-facing language failure merely because they use internal or English
  labels. For research-idea.v3 this explicitly includes the 15 required H2,
  five reasoning H3, section-1 and structured-abstract fields, evidence-chain
  fields, and Claim-Support schema headers. Their required scaffold occurrence
  is exempt; flag the wording only if it is copied into ordinary prose or a
  separate free-form table/caption label.
- Preserve the semantic cardinality and format of contract-fixed fields. A
  language repair for a field explicitly defined as one sentence, one question,
  or one table must remain one sentence, one question, or one table; reduce,
  reorder, or move nonessential detail rather than changing the field contract.
- Do not recommend translating or renaming a contract-fixed heading or field.
  If a fixed label itself materially impedes readers, record a non-blocking
  schema handoff rather than an author-facing language error.
- Language assessment may identify near-verbatim repetition, stacked
  qualifiers, or inconsistent lexical forms. It must not decide that two
  scientifically distinct, locally necessary conditions are redundant or
  require removal; argument function and cross-section necessity belong to
  narrative assessment.
- For an Idea dossier with one authoritative limitations location, report
  lexical duplication without prescribing which other reasoning locations
  retain the boundary or adding cross-section pointers. Narrative assessment
  owns that placement decision.
- Before a readiness decision, temporarily identify the actual core scientific
  roles present in the dossier, then compare every reader-facing name used for
  each role. Do not impose model, validation, branching, parameter-update, or
  failure-output roles on an Idea that does not contain them.
  A coherent finding names the preferred verified or descriptive form and all
  observed competing forms and locators; checking only the first occurrence is
  insufficient. In the same pass, replace project-management or software
  metaphors with the scientific condition, operation, or consequence they stand
  for. This is an in-memory completion check, not a term register or new artifact.
  Persist only actionable terminology findings; do not record every acceptable
  compact form merely to prove it was seen.
