# Proposal Background Argumentation

Use this contract for candidate path planning, formal v2 planning, and drafting a new proposal background. A user/funder structure is binding; otherwise use the functional defaults below.

## 1. Research-value opening

Open with one short sentence locating the broad scientific, clinical, engineering, social, policy, or methodological context. Move immediately to the highest-value real or scientific problem that the available evidence can support, establish its breadth or importance, then identify the most decision-relevant bottleneck that the project addresses.

Do not manufacture application urgency for theory-led work. Use a scientific or methodological bottleneck when that is the real value. Terms such as `most urgent`, `most critical`, or `core` require evidence of matching strength; otherwise use bounded wording.

For Chinese proposals without a binding template, display `1.1 选题价值` and `1.2 国内外研究现状`. Other languages and formats use functionally equivalent headings.

## 2. Candidate path generation

Generate two or three materially different paths only when each is coherent and evidence-supportable. Difference must change the organizing axis, inferential order, project mapping, or focal bottleneck—not merely rename headings or swap examples.

Valid sets may contain:

- several systematic paths with different defensible decomposition axes;
- several progressive paths with different defensible focusing sequences; or
- both systematic and progressive paths.

Never enforce one systematic plus one progressive option. Never score, rank, recommend, or create a weak foil. Compare all options on the same neutral dimensions: focus, coverage, evidence burden, traceability to research content, route clarity, tradeoffs, and loss-of-focus risk.

If only one path is defensible, do not freeze an options artifact. Return the smallest clarification request: ask the user to accept that path or supply another organizing constraint. Stop there.

## 3. Systematic mode

Start the current-status section by stating what the project will do and which distinct work areas are needed. Organize units by a defensible decomposition axis such as problem properties, system components, decision stages, evidence types, or technical constraints.

Each unit must include:

1. a heading and first-sentence claim that define its role;
2. the evidence scope needed to establish the current state;
3. the gap or key constraint revealed by that evidence;
4. an explicit landing on the corresponding project component; and
5. a final-sentence handoff to the next unit or synthesis.

Every unit must land on the project, and the units together must cover the planned research content. A literature taxonomy without project landing is incomplete.

## 4. Progressive mode

Choose a sequence in which each unit narrows or transforms the question so that the progression itself recovers the project's research route. For every unit, record the first-sentence claim, evidence scope, gap/constraint, project mapping, and final-sentence handoff.

The unit headings, first sentences, and final sentences must form a recoverable logical chain when read alone. The sequence must end at the project's focal problem and show how its key technical question will be addressed. A sequence that merely moves from old to new literature without an inferential dependency is not progressive mode.

## 5. Synthesis

Close the background with a short synthesis that converts the established gap into the project: project summary, route or method at decision level, innovation position, and significance. Under the default Chinese structure, this paragraph may be unnumbered and begins with `综上`; a binding template or another language may use a functional equivalent.

Do not repeat later method details or introduce new evidence in the synthesis. Its job is the `gap -> project route -> innovation position -> significance` handoff.

## 6. Formal-plan mapping

The selected path must map to:

- research contents or work packages;
- the research route or dependency order;
- key scientific, methodological, or technical problems; and
- the evidence needed for every opening and current-status claim.

The v2 content plan records this under `background_argumentation`. If the user asks to combine options, first create a new candidate round and test the hybrid's end-to-end coherence; do not concatenate units directly.
