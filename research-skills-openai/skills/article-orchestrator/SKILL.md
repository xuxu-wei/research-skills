# Research Article Development

Turn the available study record into an accurate, readable manuscript. Own the outline, drafting, revisions, and requested supporting documents.

## Approach

1. Read the available protocol, methods, results, tables, figures, analysis notes, and draft. Establish the intended contribution, audience, and article type. Identify gaps that prevent a defensible account of the study.
2. Trace the main claims to the actual evidence. Distinguish planned and exploratory analyses, observation and interpretation, and association and causation. Use available tools to verify literature and source details where needed.
3. Outline and draft an argument appropriate to the study and audience. Report what was done and found without manufacturing methods, results, citations, ethical approvals, or author declarations. Consult [manuscript guidance](references/manuscript-guidance.md) for reporting and submission materials.
4. Reconcile the text with tables, figures, and supplements. Use $methodology-statistics-preflight for unresolved design or analysis questions; use $medical-journal-review when a medical manuscript needs that additional perspective.
5. Have a fresh instance use $article-evaluator to review the current manuscript with the source results, relevant evidence, and supplied journal requirements. Do not prime it with a preferred verdict or the drafter's private reasoning.
6. Revise supported findings and retain justified disagreements. A scientific change to methods, results, or interpretation requires a new version and fresh independent review. Language and layout refinements do not require a full new review unless they change meaning.
7. Deliver the manuscript, source pointers, review, and requested supporting materials. Make remaining scientific issues and author confirmations visible.

## Completion

Missing evidence may justify a narrower claim or a clearly marked incomplete draft. Do not quietly perform new analyses or extend the study beyond the authorized task; explain the need and obtain the relevant data or decision.

Use Markdown unless another format is requested and can be produced with available tools. If independent review or formatting tools are unavailable, deliver usable material with that limitation stated. Stop when remaining work requires new study evidence, author decisions, or resources. External submission requires explicit user instruction.
