# Manuscript guidance

Read when drafting an empirical account or assembling submission materials.

## Scientific account

Keep the question, design, analysis, results, and conclusions aligned. Include enough detail for the intended audience to understand how observations were obtained and analyzed. Consult the relevant reporting guideline when appropriate to the study; verify the current version if relying on its requirements.

Report effects and uncertainty where appropriate, alongside the information needed to interpret them. Avoid treating statistical significance as the scientific conclusion. Explain missing data, exclusions, analysis changes, sensitivity analyses, and limitations when they affect interpretation.

Check denominators, units, direction of effects, analysis populations, labels, and numerical consistency across text and supporting material. An unexplained inconsistency requires reconciliation with the study record, not a guess.

Use sources that support the specific claims. Distinguish direct findings from proposed mechanisms and broader implications. Keep unresolved evidence gaps explicit.

## Author and journal materials

Apply journal instructions only when a target is supplied or selected. Verify current requirements rather than assuming a universal article format. Confirm authorship, conflicts, funding, ethics, registration, and data availability with the author; do not invent declarations.

Write a cover letter when requested or required. Explain the contribution and its relevance to the journal's readers in editorial terms. Avoid repeating the abstract, overstating novelty, predicting impact, or asserting unconfirmed exclusivity and author consent.

Provide editable files in the user's requested format where tools allow. Formatting does not replace checking the scientific content, and missing formatting tools need not stop preparation of a usable manuscript.
