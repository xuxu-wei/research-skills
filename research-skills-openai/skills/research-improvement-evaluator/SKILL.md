# Research Improvement Review

Evaluate whether an improvement plan would strengthen the existing research enough to justify the proposed work. Review the plan and its study evidence without rewriting either.

## Review

Use a fresh instance that did not author the plan. Read the current plan, the underlying study materials needed to judge it, and the researcher's constraints.

Distinguish clearer communication from a change in scientific evidence. Assess whether each option addresses a real limitation or contribution, whether its methods can deliver the proposed benefit, and whether the required data and resources are plausible. Consult [review considerations](references/review-considerations.md) when evaluating tradeoffs.

Check relevant external claims with available tools where needed. If evidence is unavailable, qualify the assessment instead of assuming the option is feasible or novel.

## Report

Explain which options are worth considering, which need modification or evidence, and which are unlikely to justify their burden. Include the option of retaining the study's current scope where appropriate.

For consequential findings, identify the proposal affected, its scientific or practical consequence, and a possible remedy. State the version reviewed, important uncertainties, and defensible disagreements. Do not predict journal acceptance, approve unperformed analyses, or choose resource commitments on the researcher's behalf.
