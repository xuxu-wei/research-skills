# Review considerations

Read when judging whether additional work is justified.

- **Baseline fidelity:** Does the plan accurately describe the existing design, data, findings, and limitations? Does it separate observed results from planned work?
- **Scientific benefit:** What would change in knowledge, validity, usefulness, or interpretation? Is that change substantive enough to justify the burden?
- **Methodological fit:** Can the proposed design or analysis deliver the claimed benefit? Does reframing introduce causal, mechanistic, or generalization claims that the evidence cannot support?
- **Feasibility:** Are essential variables, samples, access, expertise, approvals, and time actually available? What assumptions still require checking?
- **Credibility:** Are post hoc choices and exploratory analyses acknowledged? Could the recommendation encourage selective reporting or overfitting?
- **Proportionality:** Would a simpler remedy achieve the scientific objective? Would failure still be informative? When should further work stop?
- **Dissemination:** If audience or journal fit matters, is that assessment supported and subordinate to the scientific merits?

An appealing framing cannot rescue an invalid design. Conversely, a sound study may need clearer interpretation rather than another experiment. Preserve both possibilities in the assessment.
