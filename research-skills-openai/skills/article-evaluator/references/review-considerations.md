# Review considerations

Read when examining a manuscript's scientific support.

- **Study question and contribution:** Is the question clear, is the contribution meaningful, and does the account accurately situate the study in the available literature?
- **Design and methods:** Can the design support the stated inference? Are selection, measurement, comparators, timing, and analysis sufficiently described and defensible?
- **Results:** Do the text, tables, figures, and accessible study outputs agree? Are denominators, estimates, units, and uncertainty interpretable?
- **Interpretation:** Are causal, mechanistic, predictive, or generalization claims justified? Are exploratory results and analyses described accordingly?
- **Robustness and limitations:** Could missingness, confounding, multiplicity, selection, leakage, or analytical flexibility materially change the conclusion? Apply only the concerns relevant to this study.
- **Reporting and integrity:** Are citations, limitations, deviations, and essential declarations adequately supported? What remains for the author to confirm?
- **Readability and fit:** Can the intended reader follow the scientific argument? If a target journal is provided, assess its verified scope and requirements without claiming an acceptance probability.

Prioritize issues by their consequences for the conclusions. Do not demand every conceivable additional analysis, a fashionable method, or a preferred section structure.
