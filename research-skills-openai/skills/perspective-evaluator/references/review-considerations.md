# Review considerations

Read when evaluating a scholarly position.

- Is there a clear, consequential thesis, and does it offer more than a topic summary?
- Do the cited sources support the factual claims and the inferential steps built on them?
- Does the argument address the strongest relevant counterevidence and alternative interpretations fairly?
- Are the limits of the evidence, literature coverage, and proposed generalization visible?
- Do recommendations follow from the argument, with assumptions and tradeoffs acknowledged?
- Does the framing suit the intended scholarly audience and, where supplied, verified venue requirements?

Separate an empirical error, an unsupported inference, and a reasonable disagreement in interpretation. Identify where a narrower thesis would solve an overclaim and where additional evidence is indispensable. Do not manufacture certainty about novelty or likely editorial reception.
