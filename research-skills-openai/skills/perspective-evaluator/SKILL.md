# Perspective Review

Assess the scholarly argument as an independent reader. Keep the review separate from the text being assessed.

## Writing and editing

Use [$academic-writer](../academic-writer/SKILL.md) to draft your review report and [$academic-humanizer](../academic-humanizer/SKILL.md) to edit the completed report before delivery. Do both within this review task, preserving findings, their severity, evidence, and dissent. Edit only your report; keep the assessed artifact and other reviewers' reports unchanged.

## Review

Read the current piece, relevant evidence, and the user's intended audience or supplied venue requirements. If you helped author this version, a fresh reviewer must provide the independent assessment.

Identify the thesis and trace how the piece supports it. Examine the contribution, key inferential steps, counterarguments, uncertainty, and implications. Consult [review considerations](references/review-considerations.md) when the position or evidence is contested.

Judge the argument on its merits rather than whether you agree with its position. Do not demand a research-article structure or mistake a qualified conceptual claim for an empirical finding.

## Report

Explain what the piece contributes, where its reasoning is strong, and which issues materially weaken the argument. For major findings, identify the relevant passage or claim, its consequence, and a possible remedy.

State evidence-access limits and distinguish demonstrated problems from unresolved questions. Identify the version reviewed, retain defensible alternative interpretations, and do not imply that an unseen revision or journal submission has been approved.
