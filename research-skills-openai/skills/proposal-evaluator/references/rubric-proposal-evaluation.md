# Proposal Evaluation Rubric

Use a 1–5 score for each dimension. Use half-points only when justified. The evaluator must explain every score with evidence from the proposal file.

## Dimensions and Weights

- Novelty: 15%
- Feasibility: 20%
- Impact: 20%
- Relevance: 10%
- Clarity: 20%
- Completion: 15%

## Scoring Anchors

### 5 Excellent
The proposal is specific, defensible, internally coherent, and ready for high-level review. Weaknesses are minor and do not affect execution or reviewability.

### 4 Good
The proposal is generally strong and reviewable, with limited fixable issues. Core logic, methods, and contribution are mostly clear.

### 3 Adequate but Needs Revision
The proposal has a plausible direction but contains material gaps, ambiguities, or weak justification that should be revised before panel review.

### 2 Weak
The proposal has serious problems in framing, methods, feasibility, evidence, or alignment. Major revision is required before meaningful review.

### 1 Not Viable
The proposal is not currently reviewable. Core research question, method, evidence, feasibility, or relevance is absent or fundamentally flawed.

## Dimension Definitions

### Novelty
Assess whether the proposal presents a defensible original contribution after specification. Check whether the gap is real, the contribution is distinct, and the novelty claim is not overstated.

### Feasibility
Assess whether the proposed work can be executed under the stated data, methods, timeline, resources, operational constraints, and implementation conditions.

### Impact
Assess whether successful completion would have meaningful scientific, clinical, engineering, social, policy, or methodological value.

### Relevance
Assess whether the proposal matches the user goal, intended output, field context, funding or institutional requirements, and practical constraints.

### Clarity
Assess whether the research question, aims, study object, variables, endpoints, methods, and expected outputs are clearly stated and internally consistent.

### Completion
Assess whether the proposal contains the required sections, sufficient background, aims, methods, feasibility analysis, innovation, work plan, expected outputs, limitations, and references or evidence notes.

## Weighted Overall

Weighted overall should be calculated conceptually from the six dimensions. Do not allow a high weighted score to override fatal flaws or hard gate failure.
