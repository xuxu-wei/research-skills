# Review considerations

Read when assessing the scientific case and its implementation.

- **Significance and evidence:** Does the background establish a consequential problem with sources that support the claims? Does the proposal acknowledge relevant contrary evidence?
- **Aims and argument:** Does each aim follow from the problem, and would its answer support the promised contribution? Does the argument remain understandable without private planning notes?
- **Methods and inference:** Do measurements, comparisons, timing, sampling, and analysis address the aims? Are causal or predictive claims justified by the design?
- **Feasibility:** Are access, expertise, approvals, schedule, budget, and recruitment credible? Which essential dependencies remain unconfirmed?
- **Risk and alternatives:** Can the project still produce useful knowledge if an assumption fails or a result is null? Are the aims dependent in ways that threaten the whole project?
- **Application fit:** Where a call is supplied, does the proposal meet its scientific scope and material requirements? Distinguish verified requirements from inferred preferences.

Evaluate the functional argument rather than a preferred heading order. Strong prose cannot repair a design that cannot answer its central question; imperfect prose need not invalidate sound science.
