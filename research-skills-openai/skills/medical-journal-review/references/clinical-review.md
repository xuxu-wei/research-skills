# Clinical review considerations

Read when the study's clinical design or claims need closer examination.

- **Clinical question:** Are the population, treatment or exposure, comparator, outcome, and timing clear? Does the endpoint matter to patients or clinical decisions?
- **Validity:** Consider randomization or confounding, selection, measurement, missingness, follow-up, and analytical assumptions as appropriate to the design.
- **Interpretation:** Examine effect size and uncertainty, absolute as well as relative effects where relevant, and clinical importance. A small p-value does not establish clinical benefit; a nonsignificant result does not establish equivalence.
- **Causal language:** Observational associations need a defensible design and assumptions for causal interpretation. Mechanistic explanations should be qualified when they were not tested.
- **Prediction:** Discrimination is different from calibration, transportability, and clinical usefulness. Examine leakage, validation, and whether evaluation matches the claimed use.
- **Transparency:** Distinguish prespecified from exploratory analyses. Check registration, protocol deviations, missing data, selective reporting, and essential declarations when applicable.
- **Applicability:** Consider population, setting, treatment context, and the consequences of applying the result elsewhere.

Use reporting guidance appropriate to the study and check current requirements when relying on them. Do not impose a journal-specific checklist without a relevant target or make unsupported claims about its editorial preferences.
