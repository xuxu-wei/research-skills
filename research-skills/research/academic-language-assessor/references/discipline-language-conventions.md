# Discipline Language Conventions

Tense, voice, register, abbreviation, and formatting conventions by discipline. These are defaults — journal-specific author instructions may override them.

---

## 1. Biomedical / Clinical Research

### Tense by Section

| Section | Primary Tense | Notes |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| Title | Present (declarative) or past (for specific findings) | "Aspirin Reduces..." vs "Aspirin Reduced..." |
| Abstract: Background | Present | Established context |
| Abstract: Methods | Past | What was done |
| Abstract: Results | Past | What was found |
| Abstract: Conclusions | Present | What it means now |
| Introduction: Known facts | Present | Established knowledge |
| Introduction: Prior studies | Past or present perfect | "Smith et al. reported..." / "has been shown" |
| Introduction: Gap | Present | "remains unclear", "is unknown" |
| Introduction: This study | Past or present | "We investigated..." / "Here we report..." |
| Methods | Past | What was done; passive voice common but active acceptable |
| Results | Past | What was found; no interpretation |
| Discussion: Main findings | Past or present | "We found..." / "Our results demonstrate..." |
| Discussion: Comparison | Past for prior work; present for interpretation | |
| Discussion: Implications | Present; modal verbs for speculation | "may", "might", "could" |
| Discussion: Conclusions | Present | What is now known |

### Voice

- Passive voice acceptable and traditional in Methods; active voice increasingly accepted
- Active voice preferred in Introduction and Discussion
- "We" generally acceptable; "I" rare except in single-author papers or acknowledgments

### Abbreviations

- Define at first use in Abstract
- Re-define at first use in main text
- Do not define standard units (mg, mL, kg, h, min)
- Do not abbreviate if used < 3 times in the manuscript

### Numbers

- Numerals for all measurements, statistics, ages, times, dates
- Spell out numbers at the start of a sentence
- One to nine may be spelled out in running text if not a measurement

### Register

- Formal, objective
- No contractions
- No rhetorical questions in Methods/Results
- Avoid "remarkably", "strikingly", "interestingly"
- "Significant" only with P value or CI; otherwise "substantial", "marked", "notable"

## 2. Computer Science / AI / Engineering

### Tense by Section

| Section | Primary Tense | Notes |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| Introduction | Present (problem); past (prior work) | |
| Related Work | Present perfect or present | "has been applied to..." |
| System / Model Description | Present | "The model consists of..." |
| Algorithms | Present | "Algorithm 1 describes..." |
| Experimental Setup | Past | "We trained on..." |
| Results | Past for findings; present for tables/figures | "Table 1 shows..." but "Model A outperformed..." |
| Discussion / Analysis | Present for interpretation; past for results | |
| Conclusion | Present | |

### Voice

- Active voice strongly preferred
- "We" standard for describing authors' choices and methods
- Passive voice acceptable for experimental procedures

### Abbreviations

- Widely-known acronyms (CNN, LSTM, GPU, API, AUC) may be used without definition
- Domain-specific or novel acronyms must be defined at first use
- Acronym-heavy papers (> 15 acronyms) should include an acronym table

### Numbers

- Numerals throughout
- Percentages as numerals with "%" sign
- Decimal precision appropriate to the measurement

### Register

- Formal but more direct than biomedical
- "We" and active constructions are standard
- "Significantly" only with statistical test; otherwise specify the magnitude

## 3. Mathematics / Theoretical Sciences

### Tense by Section

| Section | Primary Tense | Notes |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| Introduction | Present | |
| Definitions | Present | "Let X be...", "We define..." |
| Theorems / Lemmas | Present | "Theorem 1 states..." |
| Proofs | Present | "Assume...", "It follows that..." |
| Examples / Counterexamples | Present or past | |
| Discussion | Present | |
| Conclusion | Present | |

### Voice

- "We" standard for authors
- Active voice common in proofs
- Passive voice acceptable ("it can be shown that...")

### Notation

- All non-standard notation must be defined before first use
- Consistent use of notation throughout — redefinition is an error
- Notation table recommended for symbol-heavy papers

### Register

- Precise, technical
- Hedging through mathematical conditions, not vague language
- "Clearly", "obviously", "trivially" used only when genuinely immediate

## 4. Social Sciences (APA 7th Edition)

### Tense by Section

| Section | Primary Tense | Notes |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| Introduction / Literature Review | Past or present perfect for prior studies | "Smith (2020) found..." |
| Method | Past | |
| Results | Past | |
| Discussion | Present for interpretation; past for own findings | |

### Voice

- Active voice preferred per APA 7th
- "We" acceptable for multi-author; "I" acceptable for single-author
- Passive voice only when the actor is unknown or irrelevant

### Bias-Free Language

- Use person-first or identity-first language per the population's preference
- Avoid gendered language when referring to people in general
- Specify race/ethnicity only when scientifically relevant
- Use "participants" rather than "subjects" (except in specific experimental paradigms)

### Numbers

- Numerals for numbers ≥ 10
- Spell out numbers < 10 (except measurements, statistics, ages, times)
- Percentages: use "%" with numeral

### Register

- Formal but direct
- No contractions
- Hedging appropriate to the strength of evidence

## 5. Humanities

### Tense by Section

| Section | Primary Tense | Notes |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| Text analysis / Interpretation | Present | "the author argues", "the text reveals" |
| Historical events | Past | |
| Argument / Thesis | Present | |
| Engagement with secondary literature | Present | "Smith contends that..." |

### Voice

- Active voice standard
- First person may be acceptable (discipline- and journal-dependent)
- "This paper argues" vs "I argue" — discipline preference varies

### Register

- Formal academic register
- More tolerance for stylistic variation than STEM
- Rhetorical questions may be acceptable in argumentative sections (not in descriptive sections)

---

## 6. General Science (default when discipline unclear)

- **Methods**: Past tense; passive or active voice
- **Results**: Past tense; objective reporting
- **Introduction**: Present for established facts; past or present perfect for prior work
- **Discussion**: Mixed; past for own findings, present for interpretation, modals for speculation
- **All abbreviations**: Defined at first use in abstract and main text
- **Register**: Conservative academic; no contractions, no colloquialisms, no rhetorical questions in Methods/Results
- **Numbers**: Numerals for measurements and statistics; follow journal style for running text

---

## Cross-Cutting Rules (all disciplines)

1. **Be consistent within a paragraph**: If describing a single temporal frame, do not shift between past and present without reason.
2. **When referring to figures/tables**: Present tense ("Figure 1 shows", "Table 2 presents").
3. **When referring to equations**: Present tense ("Equation 1 defines", "as shown in Eq. 2").
4. **When citing**: Past tense for findings of a specific study ("Smith et al. reported"); present perfect for a body of work ("has been established"); present tense for a paper's argument or claim ("Smith argues that").
5. **Journal-specific exceptions**: If the target journal's author instructions specify a different convention, the journal convention takes precedence.
