# Evidence Source Priority

Use this file to rank and interpret evidence sources. Do not treat source priority as idea quality.

## Priority Order

1. User-provided high-quality literature review or evidence brief.
2. Guidelines, consensus statements, regulatory documents, or professional society statements.
3. Systematic reviews, scoping reviews, meta-analyses, and authoritative narrative reviews.
4. High-quality frontier studies with clear methods and relevance.
5. Foundational papers repeatedly appearing in citation graphs.
6. Single research articles.
7. Preprints.
8. Blogs, media, forum posts, product pages, and non-peer-reviewed commentary.

## Rules

- User-provided materials are primary intake, but still label limitations.
- A single research article can generate clues, not high-confidence gaps.
- Preprints can indicate frontier direction, but usually require external verification.
- Non-peer-reviewed sources may inform terminology or practical context, not evidence confidence.
- Access failure is an evidence limitation, not negative evidence.
