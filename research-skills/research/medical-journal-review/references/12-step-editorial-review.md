# 12-Step Editorial Review Framework (Proposal / Manuscript)

This is an alternate review framework used in practice for reviewing research proposals and manuscripts from a BMJ/Nature Medicine/JAMA editorial perspective. It complements the 10-step design review process in the main SKILL.md. Use this framework when reviewing:

- Postdoc research proposals (for assessing competitiveness and readiness)
- Manuscripts being positioned for IF>10 general medical journals
- Paired proposal strategy evaluations (e.g., I201 + I202 companion papers)

Use the 10-step process in SKILL.md when the focus is on **redesign and methodology reconstruction**. Use this 12-step process when the focus is on **editorial judgment and publishability assessment**.

---

## Step 1: Information Completeness

Check whether the proposal/manuscript contains all necessary information for editorial review. Flag missing elements and rate: COMPLETE / PARTIALLY COMPLETE / INCOMPLETE.

Checklist:
- Research question (PICO-mappable)
- Hypotheses (directional, pre-specified)  
- Methods description (sufficient for reproducibility)
- Data sources (realistic, accessible)
- Endpoint definitions (primary, secondary, operationalized)
- Sample size / power justification
- Analysis plan (pre-specified)
- Publication feasibility
- Ethics statement (hard requirement for IF>10)
- Data/code sharing plan (hard requirement for IF>10)

## Step 2: Research Type and Claim Type Identification

Classify the research type and identify each claim's type. Flag MISALIGNMENT between claim ambition and design support. Flag OVERCLAIMING.

Claim types: descriptive, methodological, comparative, predictive, value/paradigm.

## Step 3: Structured Design Summary

Produce a concise structural diagram showing: design type, phases/factors/levels, comparators (fair vs. straw-man), key strengths, key weaknesses, and whether the design can answer the research question.

## Step 4: Endpoint-Analysis Alignment

Check: every endpoint has a corresponding analysis → every analysis maps to a pre-specified endpoint → success criteria are pre-specified and operationalized → no post-hoc switching implied.

## Step 5: Statistical Concerns

Flag material statistical concerns: assumption violations, estimation-vs-inference framing mismatch, multiplicity, missing data, model specification risks, simulation adequacy (MC replications, parameter space), whether the approach can deliver what claims require.

## Step 6: Generalizability Assessment

Judge result generalizability beyond study conditions: population/setting/condition coverage, simulation-to-real-world gap, known limits, unanticipated limits.

## Step 7: Ethics and Data Sharing

For IF>10 journals, these are HARD requirements: ethics statement (even for secondary data), data sharing plan (repository, timeline, access), code availability (repository, license), registration (PROSPERO, OSF, clinicaltrials.gov if applicable).

## Step 8: Journal Fit and Competitiveness

Assess: appropriate journal tier, what makes this competitive, what weakens it, realistic primary target, stretch target.

## Step 9: Reviewer Prediction

Predict reviewer reactions by type: methodologist/statistician, clinical domain expert, general medical reader/editor. For each: likely praise, likely criticism, request type.

## Step 10: Editorial Recommendation

One of: ACCEPT / MINOR REVISION / MAJOR REVISION / REJECT. Grounded in preceding steps, not vague impressions.

## Step 11: Scoring (100-point scale)

### Dimensions (proposal-optimized variant)

| Dimension | Max | Focus |
|-----------|-----|-------|
| Innovation / Novelty | 20 | Is this genuinely novel or incremental? |
| Design-Question Alignment | 20 | Can the design actually answer the question? |
| Clinical Significance | 15 | Will this change practice if successful? |
| Methodological Rigor | 15 | Are methods appropriate and well-specified? |
| Feasibility / Executability | 15 | Can this be completed with available resources? |
| Presentation / Clarity | 15 | Is the proposal clear and well-structured? |
| **Total** | **100** | |

Interpretation:
- 85-100: Tier 1 journal competitive
- 70-84: Tier 2 journal appropriate; Tier 1 stretch
- 55-69: Publishable but low top-journal competitiveness
- 40-54: Major reconstruction needed
- <40: Does not support high-impact publication

### vs. SKILL.md Rubric

The SKILL.md uses a different rubric (Scientific question importance 20, Evidence gap 15, Design-question fit 20, Clinical significance 15, Bias control 15, Transparency 10, Journal narrative 5). Both are valid. Use the SKILL.md rubric when the review is methodology-reconstruction focused; use this rubric when the review is editorial-publishability focused.

## Step 12: Revision Priorities

If not ACCEPT, list ordered priorities:
1. **Fatal / blocking** — must fix before submission
2. **Major** — will trigger rejection if unaddressed
3. **Minor** — polish before submission

Each item must be specific and actionable.
