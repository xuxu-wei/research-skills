# Contribution Types

Choose one primary type and at most one secondary type.

- Reframing: changes how the field should define a problem.
- Synthesis: connects scattered evidence into a new interpretation.
- Critique: shows why a dominant assumption or practice is flawed.
- Agenda-setting: identifies the next important questions or actions.
- Translational: explains what findings imply for practice, policy, or implementation.
- Ethical-social: clarifies normative stakes, governance, or social impact.
- Methods perspective: explains how methodological choices shape the field.

The primary type must appear in the argument skeleton and drive the opening, evidence selection, and conclusion.
