# Proposal Fatal Flaws

A fatal flaw is a defect that prevents the proposal from being accepted unless repaired. Some fatal flaws are repairable; others require rejection or return to idea refinement.

## Common Fatal Flaws

- Research question is not answerable.
- Primary study object, population, system, dataset, or phenomenon is undefined.
- Core aim and method do not match.
- Proposed data cannot support the stated research question.
- Key variables, endpoint, outcome, metric, or deliverable cannot be defined.
- Major confounding, bias, or validity risk is acknowledged but not controllable.
- Novelty claim is not credible after specification.
- Proposal depends on data, access, equipment, collaborators, or permissions not plausibly available.
- Ethical, privacy, regulatory, or implementation blocker prevents execution.
- Proposal is misaligned with the user's intended output or stated constraints.
- Proposal lacks enough structure to be evaluated.

## Repairability

Classify each fatal flaw as:

- repairable by clarification;
- repairable by targeted revision;
- repairable only by major reframing;
- not repairable within the current proposal.

If a fatal flaw is not repairable within the current proposal, recommend `reject` or return to idea refinement.
